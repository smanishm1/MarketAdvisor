How This Works, In Plain English

Each week (Friday), the system ranks the 11 SPDR sector ETFs by a blend of 3-month and 6-month momentum. It wants to hold the top 3 sectors, but only if a sector is also beating the S&P 500 (SPY) and trading above its own long-term trend average -- if fewer than 3 sectors qualify, the rest of the money simply stays in cash. Money is split by conviction: the #1-ranked sector gets the biggest slice, decaying down the list, and no single sector can ever be more than a capped share of the portfolio. The system watches daily for a reason to sell (a sector falling out of favor, breaking its trend, or hitting a hard stop-loss) and acts on those exits automatically -- but it never buys anything without your click. If the market falls sharply, built-in circuit breakers pause new buys or move everything to cash automatically, and only resume once conditions improve or you click Resume. Every so often the system reviews its own results and may suggest one small tuning change -- again, only with your approval, and only after showing you a historical backtest of the idea.

Specs / Behavior Reference

      

        Risk controls
        
    
          
  • Black-swan brake: SPY down ≥7% in a day, or portfolio drawdown ≥20% from peak → flattens to cash and halts new buys. Auto-resumes when SPY closes back above its 200-day average, or via manual Resume.
    • 
          
  • Early brake: SPY down ≥5% in a day → pauses new buys only (no selling). Clears automatically on recovery.
    • 
          
  • Per-position catastrophe stop: any holding down ≥stop% from its own entry → sold automatically.
    • 
          
  • Intraday risk overlay polls live prices every ~20s so stops/breakers react in minutes; all ranking/rebalance decisions still use daily closes only.
    • 
          
  • No margin: the book can never go cash-negative; new buy proposals are capped by cash minus already-pending proposals.
    • 
          
  • Approved buys are filled at the current market price at approval time, preserving the dollar amount budgeted.
    • 
        

      

      

        Reflection loop
        
    
          
  • Reviews the last N closed trades (default 5) and proposes AT MOST one change to ONE dial: trend-filter length, exit-rank hysteresis, catastrophe stop %, or position cap %.
    • 
          
  • Below the minimum sample size, it always holds -- no tuning on noise.
    • 
          
  • Every proposal can be backtested (full history + held-out out-of-sample window) before you approve it.
    • 
          
  • A dial value you've already rejected is never re-proposed.
    • 
          
  • The strategy's structure (universe, sizing, position count, cadence) is locked and cannot be changed by this loop.
